Findings (2026-11-06):
1. Global standardisation (pooling all 40 components) is the main win: the direct
   map's log-spectrum correlation jumps 0.425 -> 0.678 (+60%) at lr=3e-3, and the
   one-step NRMSE also improves slightly (0.8035 -> 0.7990).
2. Larger lr (1e-2 vs 3e-3) does NOT help: spectrum correlation drops for both
   modes (direct 0.678->0.549, delta 0.647->0.463). Keep lr=3e-3.
3. The delta codec does NOT improve spectrum statistics -- it is worse on every
   spectrum metric at both learning rates (its only edge: free-running NRMSE
   1.07-1.09 vs 1.12-1.13, which reflects stronger contraction towards the mean,
   rms ratio 0.59-0.61 vs 0.67, itself a defect rather than a virtue).
   Why: with global standardisation the direct target z(t+dt) is already O(1) with
   the same spectral content; the increment removes the shared identity component
   but forces the network to synthesise the full increment from scratch, and the
   resulting decoded state accumulates a larger mean drift (mean ratio 1.26-1.33
   vs 1.18-1.25). The 'learn fluctuations not offsets' argument only pays off when
   the offset dwarfs the fluctuations *in the loss*, which global standardisation
   already removes by construction.
